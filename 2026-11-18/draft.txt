Question 1: Origins and Dual Structure of the Feudal World

According to the text, what geopolitical crisis forced Western Europe to develop feudalism 
in the ninth century? Additionally, how did the relationship between lords and vassals 
(political/military) differ from the relationship between lords and serfs (economic/manorial)?

The crisis in the ninth century was generated by the death of Charlemagne that led to the fracture
of the Carolingian Empire. Western Europe had strong problems in both political fragmentation and
external vulnerability. Out of sovereign authority emerged feudalism, at its core the feudal order
guaranteed land in return for military service and loyalty.
In terms of political/military relationship the Lords like Monarchs granted large tracts of territory
(fiefs) to powerful his vassals through a formal ceremony of homage and the recipient became a vassal,
kneeling before his lord and swearing an oath of fealty.
The economic engine was led by the manorial system. Manorialism organized rural life and agricultural
production on each individual estate. The vast majority of the population consisted of serfs.
They owed the lord several days of unpaid labor each week, a steep portion of their harvest and fees.
In exchange serfs were bound to the soil for life, besides they were guaranteed with physical
protection behind the castle walls.
